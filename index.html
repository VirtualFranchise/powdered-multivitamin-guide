Who it's for

Who is it for, and who should ask a doctor first?

Often a good match

  • Adults who want one daily step instead of several bottles
  • People who prefer not to swallow tablets
  • People who want methylcobalamin (B12) and 5-MTHF (folate)

Ask your doctor or pharmacist first if you

  • Are pregnant, trying to conceive or breastfeeding
  • Take blood thinners, thyroid medicine or other prescriptions
  • Have kidney or liver disease, smoke, or are buying for a child
On thyroid medicine (levothyroxine)?

What is in one Isotonix® Daily Essentials packet?

One 13.4 g stick pack combines a capful each of four Isotonix® formulas: OPC-3®, Multivitamin, Activated B-Complex and Calcium Plus. The label lists 12 vitamins, 11 minerals (including small amounts of sodium and boron) and five plant extracts at 25 mg each.

How do you take it?

The directions say to pour one packet into 8 oz of water, stir thoroughly, and “Enjoy once daily; maximum absorption occurs when taken on an empty stomach.”

Does it contain iron?

No. Iron is not on the Supplement Facts panel. If your doctor told you to take iron, you would need a separate source.

Does one packet cover 100% of every vitamin and mineral?

No. All 12 listed vitamins are at or above 100% of the adult RDA. Several minerals are partial: calcium about 44%, zinc 68%, magnesium 30%, potassium 12% and copper 11% of the adult RDA or AI.

How much sugar is in it?

Each packet has 25 calories and 6 g of sugars, 5 g of them added (fructose and glucose). It is also sweetened with stevia leaf extract and monk fruit.

Is any nutrient above an NIH upper limit?

Niacin is listed at 60 mg (as niacin and niacinamide). NIH's upper limit for niacin from supplements is 35 mg and applies to both forms. If you take other products with niacin, review your total with your doctor.
