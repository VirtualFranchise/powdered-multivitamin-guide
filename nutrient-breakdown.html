How to read it

Notes on specific nutrients

Is 60 mg of niacin too much?

NIH sets a 35 mg upper limit for niacin from supplements, based mainly on flushing from the nicotinic-acid form, and states it applies to both forms. The label lists 60 mg as niacin and niacinamide without splitting them. Taking other niacin products, or have liver disease or gout? Review your total with your doctor (NIH ODS).

Is the vitamin A (beta-carotene) a concern?

NIH's vitamin A upper limit covers only preformed vitamin A; none is set for beta-carotene. Two large trials found high-dose beta-carotene (20–30 mg a day) raised lung cancer risk in smokers, and the USPSTF recommends against beta-carotene supplements. This packet's amount is roughly 6 mg. Smokers should ask their doctor (NIH ODS).

Is the folate within limits?

650 mcg DFE as (6S)-5-methyltetrahydrofolate. NIH's 1,000 mcg upper limit is set for folic acid; whether it applies to 5-MTHF, and a formal conversion factor, have not been established (NIH ODS).

Does the biotin affect lab tests?

High-dose biotin can interfere with some lab tests, including thyroid and troponin tests. This packet has 600 mcg. FDA advises telling your doctor about any biotin before blood work (NIH ODS).

Why is B12 at 8,125%?

The label lists 195 mcg against a 2.4 mcg RDA. NIH has set no upper limit for vitamin B12.

What about the pine bark extract?

Each packet has 25 mg. If you take blood thinners, ask your doctor first. See the safety notes.

Why doesn't the label %DV match?

The FDA Daily Value is one number for every label; the RDA varies by age and sex. Vitamin D: the DV is 20 mcg (188%), while the RDA for adults under 71 is 15 mcg (250%).

Is more than 100% a problem?

Not by itself. For thiamin, riboflavin, B12, biotin and pantothenic acid, NIH set no upper limit. The number that matters for safety is the upper limit, where one exists.

FAQ
How much vitamin D is in one packet?

37.5 mcg (1,500 IU) of vitamin D3. That is 250% of the adult RDA of 15 mcg and 38% of NIH's 100 mcg (4,000 IU) upper limit.

Is 60 mg of niacin too much?

NIH's upper limit for niacin from supplements is 35 mg, and it applies to both niacin (nicotinic acid) and niacinamide. The label lists 60 mg of the two combined without splitting them. The limit is based mainly on skin flushing. If you take other niacin products or have liver disease or gout, review it with your doctor.

Why is the B12 percentage so high?

The label lists 195 mcg, about 8,125% of the 2.4 mcg RDA. NIH has set no upper limit for vitamin B12.

Does it contain iron or vitamin K?

Neither appears on the Supplement Facts panel.
